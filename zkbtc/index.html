Digital signatures and zero-knowledge validity proofs solve part of the problem — but the benefit is gone as long as a trusted party still orders the transactions or holds the backing bitcoin. The paper proposes a system that uses Bitcoin as an ordering layer and nothing else: every state transition carries its own recursive validity proof, and the only thing that reaches the chain is a 64-byte nullifier. zkBTC is the one-for-one backed Bitcoin token on that protocol.

Why it is worth reading: the paper derives the construction from first principles and states its assumptions in plain words instead of hiding them — 1-of-N setup honesty, at least one honest challenger acting inside the challenge window, sound circuit and graph cryptography. Anyone who wants to check whether the arithmetic holds will find everything needed to do so.

In one sentence

Not a protocol token — an asset on the protocol

zkCoins deliberately has no native token. zkBTC is a different thing: a published standard for how real Bitcoin enters the shielded pool, changes hands privately inside it, and comes back out — verified by the same circuit and the same nullifier accumulator as every other zkCoins asset.

Asset model
An ordinary zkCoins coin

zkBTC is token standard 3 on the zkCoins protocol. No second protocol, no separate chain, no consensus of its own.

Transfer
Free once minted

Any holder may send zkBTC to any address — without the gatekeeper's permission, without its signature, without it being online.

Redemption
Exit without the gatekeeper

The reserve rests on BitVM2 — no federation multisig. Exit needs one registered operator that is live, liquid and willing, in an epoch that still has vault capacity.

Gatekeeper
Entry control, never custody

An optional gatekeeper screens the source of new deposits at mint time — and has no role at peg-out.

Privacy

Public at the edges, private in between

Entry and exit are ordinary Bitcoin events and therefore visible to everyone. Everything in between is not.

How it runs

From locked bitcoin and back

Six steps. The marker on the right says whether the step lands readable on the chain or leaves nothing but a 64-byte nullifier — every state-advancing step publishes one.

  1. 01

    Deposit

    The depositor pays into a deposit address. Only once every operator of the epoch has pre-signed the spending paths does the BTC move into the vault — until then the depositor can reclaim it through the refund path.

    Readable on the chain
  2. 02

    Entry check — optional

    If the asset designates a gatekeeper, it checks the source of funds, its own view of the chain, and whether the epoch's operator set contains at least one independent honest signer, before releasing the mint. Without it no new zkBTC comes into existence — and it cannot reach the ones that already do.

    Readable on the chain
  3. 03

    Mint

    The coin appears in the pool, bound to exactly the recipient address committed beforehand. Redirecting it is ruled out.

    Only a 64-byte marker
  4. 04

    Transfer freely

    From here zkBTC is an ordinary zkCoins coin: pass it on as often as you like, without asking anyone and without anyone reading along.

    Only a 64-byte marker
  5. 05

    Announce redemption

    The holder burns the coin and names the highest fee they are willing to pay. The gatekeeper has no role here.

    Only a 64-byte marker
  6. 06

    Payout

    A registered operator fronts the BTC and reclaims it from the vault. Who takes that on is decided by the market — the holder themselves, if they registered as an operator and posted a bond before the epoch.

    Readable on the chain
Roles

Who is allowed to do what

The load-bearing rows from the specification. The telling ones are those where nobody can act — and the one right below them, which is the difference between cannot and will not.

AbilityGatekeeperOperatorHolder
Refuse a new deposit Yes Yes No
See a depositor's off-protocol identity data Yes No No
Block a transfer No No No
Freeze or seize coins No No No
Block a redemption in the protocol No No No
Decline to serve a redemption No Yes No
Redirect a mint to another address No No No
Empty the vault unilaterally No No No
Front a payout and charge a fee No Yes Yes

Cannot is not the same as will not. Nobody can block a redemption in the protocol — but the coin is burned first, and it stays unserved until some registered operator fronts the BTC within the holder's maximum fee. The specification names this case and files it under liveness, not theft. Two more conditions belong to the rows above: no operator can empty the vault as long as at least one honest challenger acts inside the challenge window and the circuit and graph cryptography is sound, and a holder can front a payout or register as an operator only after posting a bond before the epoch.

Honestly counted

Costs and residual risks

The six points that a valuation runs into first. The specification lists twelve — this is not the whole list, and the whole list is worth reading.

Residual risks

  • Conditional guarantees, not unconditional ones. "No operator can steal" holds under 1-of-N setup honesty, at least one honest challenger acting inside the challenge window, and sound circuit and BitVM2-graph cryptography. A critical soundness bug enables actual theft.
  • Liveness, not custody. Exit needs at least one registered operator that is live, liquid and willing to front BTC. A redemption burns the coin first — a fee set too low leaves it unserved until someone takes it on.
  • The gatekeeper is a real trust concentration. In gated mode a compromised or negligent gatekeeper can enable a backing drain of the entire pooled reserve. And if it withholds its mint signature after the backing transaction has fired, the depositor's already-vaulted BTC becomes an irrevocable contribution to the reserve — a loss path on the depositor's side.
  • Pooled contagion. The reserve is pooled and the token is fungible — a single drained vault dilutes every holder, not just the counterparty of one deposit.
  • The edges are public. Amounts and timing at peg-in and peg-out are observable and correlatable, even though transfers inside the pool stay shielded.
  • Burned is burned. With no security council and no admin keys, the worst case under the trust model is a freeze or burn of the affected deposits — potentially permanent.
Maturity

What is still missing

The specification names two gate families that must both clear before an asset may be called zkBTC: four maturity gates, one of which is met, and four REQ-4 gates that carry the redemption guarantee itself.

Design specification No implementation code exists yet, the design is not externally audited and not production-ready. zkBTC is a worked-out draft that names its own open questions — the specification lists fourteen of them, including one bound on the redeem side that is not yet mechanized.

  • BitVM2 running on mainnetMet

    Bitlayer's BitVM bridge since July 2025, Citrea's Clementine since January 2026. The reserve construction is no longer research.

  • Open operator marketOpen

    Bonding, registration ceremony, and a policy that keeps any single party from dominating an epoch.

  • Compliance-predicate conversionOpen

    The zkCoins predicate has to be brought into a form BitVM2 can verify at all.

  • External auditOpen

    Circuit, BitVM2 graph and pre-signed graphs, published before mainnet. Exactly the class of fault that would otherwise enable real theft — and sound circuit and graph cryptography remains a standing assumption even after the audit.

Seven of the eight are still open. Alongside the four maturity gates above stand four REQ-4 gates — anti-domination in the operator market, setup integrity of the pre-signing ceremony, documented holder-to-operator onboarding, and challenger economics. As long as one box is empty, zkBTC is a specification and not a product.

FAQ

Questions, answered

What is zkBTC?

zkBTC is token standard 3 on the zkCoins protocol: lock real Bitcoin, hold it as a private and freely transferable coin, and redeem it back into on-chain BTC without a central party's permission. Today it is a design specification — no implementation code, unaudited, not production-ready.

Is zkBTC custodial?

No. The reserve sits in vaults that can only be spent along pre-signed BitVM2 fraud-proof paths; there is no key that lets anyone move it at will. What zkBTC does depend on is liveness — redeeming needs at least one registered operator that is live, liquid and willing. And in gated mode it depends on the gatekeeper doing its entry checks honestly: that is a backing-safety dependency, not merely a liveness one.

What can the gatekeeper actually do?

It can refuse or approve new mints at the entrance and see where a deposit came from. It cannot freeze coins, block a transfer, refuse a redemption, redirect a mint, or touch the vault. It has no role at peg-out at all. In gated mode it is still a real trust concentration: a compromised or negligent gatekeeper can enable a drain of the pooled reserve.

How private is zkBTC really?

Transfers inside the shielded pool reveal no amounts, no parties and no lineage — on the chain each leaves nothing but a 64-byte nullifier. Peg-in and peg-out are ordinary Bitcoin events, so amounts and timing at the boundary are visible and can be correlated; and once an operator claims its reimbursement, that claim marker links the redemption to its payout. The specification says plainly that zkCoins with a bridge has less privacy than zkCoins without one.

Can I use zkBTC today?

No. Two gate families have to clear first. BitVM2 on mainnet is met; still open are the operator market, the conversion of the compliance predicate, an external audit of circuit and graph, and the four REQ-4 gates that carry the redemption guarantee. Until then zkBTC is a published design, not a product.

Is zkBTC a new coin?

No. Each zkBTC is minted against an equal amount of real Bitcoin locked at that moment; it is not a separate currency and not a native protocol token. zkCoins deliberately has none. That the backing holds afterwards is a conditional property, not an unconditional invariant — the conditions are listed under residual risks.
